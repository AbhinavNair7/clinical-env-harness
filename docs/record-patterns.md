# Record patterns

A library of the kinds of mess real records contain, for planting in case records (the record
layer of a sourced case, `docs/case-sourcing.md`). Types and counts only: no patient-level data.
Each case uses 2–4 of them and lists which ones in its provenance file.

Sources: the MIMIC-IV and MIMIC-IV-ED demo data (counts below, US hospital data); Edith's
`record_traits`; and the medication-discrepancy types that recur in the transitions-of-care
literature (for example the Medication Discrepancy Tool, Coleman et al. 2005). The primary
care patterns are written from clinical experience. MIMIC has no notes, so it shows how coded
and ordered data drift, not how letters read.

## Patterns to plant

| Pattern | Where it shows | Record trait | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Stopped medicine still on the list or still dispensed | primary care chart, pharmacy fill history, pill organizer | outdated_entries | Edith; auto-refill in US pharmacies |
| Hospital-started medicine with no indication or stop date (PPI, antipsychotic, sedative) carried into the discharge list | discharge summary, then the chart | contradicts_other_records | MIMIC: about half of PPIs and 60% of antipsychotics had no matching code |
| Home medicine omitted or changed in hospital, not reconciled at discharge ("resume home medicines") | discharge summary vs chart vs pharmacy | contradicts_other_records | MIMIC: most admitted older patients had a home medicine never ordered in hospital (upper bound, see below) |
| Same drug, another salt or formulation (metoprolol tartrate vs succinate) | discharge list vs fill history | contradicts_other_records | MIMIC: common at admission |
| Antihypertensive held in hospital for low blood pressure; the discharge summary does not say whether to restart | discharge summary | contradicts_itself (or silent) | MIMIC eMAR: about 1 in 10 metoprolol doses and more hydralazine doses held or not given |
| Chronic diagnosis coded once and missing the next time; problem list neither current nor complete | primary care chart problem list | miscoded, outdated_entries | MIMIC: a chronic condition vanished between consecutive admissions in about a third of pairs |
| Long, copied-forward note: last visit's template text repeated ("falls screen negative", "no concerns") | primary care chart | long_noisy, contradicts_other_records | US EHR copy-forward; Edith's cluttered record |
| Rosy templated agency or aide log ("client tolerated care well") above a free-text line that matters | home care agency notes | long_noisy | Edith |
| Duplicate therapy from two prescribers (specialist and primary care) | fill history vs chart | contradicts_other_records | transitions literature (duplication) |
| Brand and generic names for the same medicine on different lists | pharmacy vs chart vs what the patient says | contradicts_other_records | transitions literature (brand/generic confusion) |
| Dose on the label differs from the dose actually taken (patient halves tablets, skips the evening dose) | what the patient or carer says vs pharmacy | (a person's wrong or outdated fact) | transitions literature (non-intentional non-adherence) |
| EMS attended a fall and the patient declined transport; nobody told primary care | EMS patient care report | (a record the chart never mentions) | Edith (ambulance report) |
| Readmission within weeks to a different service, so the second discharge summary does not mention the first | discharge summaries | contradicts_other_records | MIMIC: 19 of 70 readmission gaps were within 30 days |
| Discharged to home health or a skilled nursing facility, then home: care plan in the agency's notes, not the chart | agency notes vs chart | contradicts_other_records | MIMIC: home health care and SNF are as common as plain home |

Rules for planting them: every wrong or outdated fact sits in a conflict (the validator checks
this); a nearly clean case uses one pattern at most; a pattern is only planted where the
clinical arc makes it matter, never as decoration.

## Counts from the MIMIC-IV demo

Generated by `uv run clinical-env sources mimic mimic/raw` from MIMIC-IV Clinical Database Demo
2.2 and MIMIC-IV-ED Demo 2.2 (`ATTRIBUTION.md`). Contains information from those databases,
which are made available under the Open Database License (ODbL v1.0).

Read with care. This is one Boston hospital and 24 patients. Name matching uses each medicine's
first word, so brand vs generic names ("depakote" vs divalproex) count as "never ordered", and
small differences in a name count as another formulation. Both of those lines are upper
bounds. "Started with no matching coded indication" means no matching ICD code at that
admission, not that the doctor had no reason.

### Admissions timeline

- Gap from discharge to the next admission (70 gaps): within 30 days 19, 31-90 days 13,
  91-365 days 17, over a year 21.
- Discharged to: home 22, home health care 20, skilled nursing facility 18, not recorded 16,
  died 9, chronic/long term acute care <5, rehab <5, hospice <5.

### Coded problem list

- Diagnosis codes per admission: median 17 (IQR 12.75-26).
- A chronic condition coded at one admission and absent at the next: 24 of 70 (34%)
  consecutive admission pairs. Most often: hypertension 10, heart failure <5, chronic kidney
  disease <5, atrial fibrillation <5.
- Admissions with a "personal history of" code: 62 of 94 (66%); with a "long-term (current)
  use of" drug code: 48 of 94 (51%).

### Hospital prescribing

- Different medicines ordered per admission: median 24 (IQR 15.75-31.25).
- Started with no matching coded indication: proton pump inhibitor with no upper GI code 19 of
  37 (51%) admissions where one was ordered; antipsychotic with no psychiatric, delirium or
  dementia code 9 of 15 (60%); insulin with no diabetes code 11 of 45 (24%).
- A benzodiazepine or Z-drug ordered: 25 of 94 (27%) admissions.

### Prescribed vs given (eMAR)

- Doses charted as not given or held: 1171 of 10325 (11%) charted doses.
- Blood pressure and heart medicines not given or held, of doses charted: metoprolol 42 of 493
  (9%), hydralazine 21 of 141 (15%), furosemide 7 of 180 (4%), lisinopril 5 of 34 (15%),
  carvedilol, amlodipine and torsemide suppressed (fewer than 5 each, of 60, 54 and 93).

### ED home-medicine list vs the admission's orders

- ED demo patients who are also in the hospital demo: 64 of 64 (100%). (Checked in the data,
  as the dataset page says.)
- Admitted ED stays of patients aged 65+ with a home-medicine list: 57.
- Home medicines never ordered during the admission: 332 of 714 (46%) listed medicines; stays
  with at least one: 52 of 57 (91%).
- Home medicine ordered under another formulation or salt (same first word, different name):
  130 medicines.
- Medicines ordered in hospital that were not on the home list, per stay: median 16 (IQR
  11-25).
