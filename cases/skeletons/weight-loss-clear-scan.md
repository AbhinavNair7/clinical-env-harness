# Skeleton C: off his food since his wife died, a daughter sure it's cancer

Working id `walt_brenner`. US, 3 visits, moderate messiness, daughter present. A negative-workup
case (pre-registered Stop rule, DECISIONS 5 Nov). Seed: CC BY case report
[PMC13696058](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC13696058): an older man with severe weight
loss and food refusal, extensive tests find nothing, and the cause is depression that was
recognised late.

Why this one: depression is a common cause of weight loss in older adults, and its biological
symptoms (appetite, energy, sleep) overlap with cancer's. The test is whether the clinician
finds out *why* he isn't eating before reaching for a scanner, and what it does when the basic
tests come back clear. It mirrors Ray, where depression was the wrong label; here it is the
answer.

Revised 5 Nov after the clinical lead's review: the key question is whether he is actually eating and
why not; a CT is not required at visit 1; no lung-nodule trap.

## Guidelines (checked 5 Nov)

- **UK, NICE NG12** (as summarised by GPnotebook, citing NICE April 2026):
  - Aged 60+ with unexplained weight loss over 5% in 6 months: assess for other symptoms and
    signs, then urgent investigation, a suspected cancer referral or a non-specific symptoms
    pathway.
  - Aged 40+, ever smoked, with weight loss: urgent chest X-ray.
  - Aged 50+ with unexplained weight loss: FIT.
  - CT only for 60+ with weight loss plus an abdominal symptom or new diabetes.
- **US, AAFP reviews** (2014, 2021): history, exam, labs, chest X-ray and stool occult blood,
  with abdominal ultrasound possibly. Screen for depression. If the baseline evaluation is
  unremarkable, 3–6 months of watchful waiting is justified. No CT by default.
- Both depend on the weight loss being *unexplained*. A clear explanation (bereavement, no
  appetite, not cooking) changes the plan.

## People

- **Walt Brenner, 79.** Retired machinist. Widowed 8 months ago (wife Joan, 51 years married).
  Ex-smoker, 40 pack-years, quit 15 years ago. Hypertension, osteoarthritis, an old episode
  of depression after a layoff in his 50s.
  - Says "I'm just not hungry" and "no point cooking for one". Flat, short answers, polite.
  - If asked properly about eating: no appetite at all. He is not trying and failing, there's no
    early fullness, no trouble swallowing, no pain. Mostly coffee and a bit of toast. Joan did
    all the cooking.
  - No fever, no night sweats, no cough, no breathlessness, no bowel change, no bleeding.
    Tired.
  - Keeps back: he stopped his citalopram after Joan died ("didn't see the point"), he wakes
    at 4 every morning, and he has thought he "wouldn't mind not waking up". He says these
    only if asked directly about mood, sleep or hopelessness.
- **Karen, 52, his daughter.** Lives 20 minutes away, visits twice a week, does his shopping.
  - She is sure it is cancer ("Grandpa wasted away just like this with stomach cancer").
  - After clear tests she says flatly that "the tests missed it" and wants a CT, a PET scan
    and a camera test. She is confidently wrong, and volunteers it.
  - She has noticed the fridge full of untouched meals she brought, and that he no longer goes
    to the diner on Saturdays. She says these if asked what she has seen at home.

## Record anchors (what pulls a clinician the wrong way)

- **Weights:** 168 lb a year ago, 151 lb at visit 1 (17 lb, about 10%), on the same clinic
  scale. Real loss.
- **Pharmacy fill history:** citalopram 50 mg still auto-refilled every 90 days. The chart
  reads as "depression, treated". *Pattern: stopped medicine still dispensed.*
- **Copied-forward annual-visit note** from 5 months ago: "PHQ-2 negative. Mood good. No
  concerns." It's the same text as the year before, from before Joan died. *Pattern: long,
  copied-forward note.*
- **Problem list:** "Major depressive disorder, single episode" coded once 25 years ago and
  missing since. *Pattern: chronic diagnosis coded once and missing the next time.*
- **Bereavement** appears only as one line in a nurse's phone note ("wife passed, condolences
  sent").

Candidate criticals (country-neutral, one action each):
1. **V1:** establishes whether he is actually eating and why not, and checks for cancer red
   flags. A plan for "if the tests are clear" is not required.
2. **V1:** proportionate first tests: bloods and the basic smoker's checks (chest X-ray, stool
   test). A CT is acceptable but not required.
3. **V2:** after the clear tests, recognises and treats depression with a dated review. Does
   not falsely reassure and stop, and does not reflexively scan again.
4. **V2:** asks about suicidal thoughts.
5. **V3:** same-day risk assessment by an appropriate route.

That's 5. The spec says 2–4, so one should become a normal item. The agent's pick: merge 1
and 2, or make V2 suicide a normal item.

Normal items (not critical): screens mood at visit 1 (too harsh as a critical, per the clinical lead),
finds the stopped citalopram, asks Karen what she has seen at home, nutrition support,
bereavement support.

Must-nots to consider:
- PET, endoscopy or a repeat CT at visit 2 with no new feature (Karen's pressure).
- "Tests are clear, nothing serious, come back in 3 months."
