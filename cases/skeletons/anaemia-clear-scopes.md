# Skeleton D: iron-deficient, the GI tests are clear, and "she only takes what the doctor gives her"

Working id `dot_fairbanks`. 3 visits, moderate messiness, husband present. A negative-workup
case (pre-registered Stop rule, DECISIONS 5 Nov). Seed: CC BY case report
[PMC13235628](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC13235628): an older man with chronic
iron-deficiency anaemia; the cause was a long-term NSAID found on medication review, and it
resolved when the NSAID was stopped.

Why this one: in older adults, the common causes of iron deficiency that GI tests do not show
are diet and medicines (an over-the-counter NSAID, aspirin on top of an anticoagulant). The
answer is in what she eats and what she actually takes.

Revised 5 Nov after the clinical lead's review: FIT first; screen the bowel and upper-GI cancer
symptoms; diet is a true, common cause alongside the NSAID; a calm visit 3 where the test is
not overdoing it.

## Guidelines (checked 5 Nov)

- **UK: BSG/ACPGBI 2025 guidance on FIT in iron-deficiency anaemia** ([Frontline Gastroenterology
  2025](https://www.bsg.org.uk/clinical-resource/role-of-fit-in-the-investigation-of-ida)):
  - FIT and coeliac serology in primary care before referral.
  - FIT at or above 10 µg/g: suspected cancer pathway for upper and lower GI investigation, same
    day.
  - FIT below threshold with no suspicious symptoms: an urgent non-cancer pathway. Secondary
    care decides whether and when to scope, based on age, Hb and comorbidities.
- **US: AGA 2020 guideline** ([AAFP summary](https://www.aafp.org/pubs/afp/issues/2021/0800/p211)):
  - Bidirectional endoscopy for men and postmenopausal women, with non-invasive coeliac and
    H. pylori tests first.
  - After negative endoscopy, a trial of iron before routine capsule endoscopy.
  - FIT is not part of the US pathway.
- **So the country matters at visit 1.** FIT-first is the UK route. Agent's pick: make Dot a UK
  case (like Edith), so FIT-first is the guideline route. Otherwise the critical has to accept
  either route.

## People

- **Dorothy "Dot" Fairbanks, 82.** Retired school secretary. Atrial fibrillation on apixaban
  (cardiology, 14 months), an old TIA, osteoarthritis of both knees, hypertension.
  - Tired and breathless on the stairs. Puts it down to "getting old".
  - **Diet:** since her knees got bad she doesn't stand to cook. Mostly tea and toast, soup,
    cereal, little meat. She says so if asked what she eats in a day.
  - **Bowel and upper-GI screen:** no change in bowel habit, no blood, no black stools, no
    weight loss, no trouble swallowing, no vomiting. Some heartburn after meals lately. She
    says these if asked.
  - **Aleve:** takes 2 tablets most days for her knees. She doesn't think of it as a medicine
    and mentions it only if asked specifically about painkillers or anything bought at the
    store.
  - Stopped the iron tablets after a week because "they bunged me up". Says so only if asked
    how she is getting on with them.
- **Frank, 84, her husband.** Does the shopping and buys the big bottle of Aleve.
  - Confidently wrong, and volunteers it: "She only takes what the doctor gives her. It's all
    in the pill box, I do it every Sunday."
  - Worried it's cancer because "her sister had bowel cancer".
  - At visit 3 he says the Aleve is fine: "it's from the grocery store, it can't hurt".

## Record anchors (what pulls a clinician the wrong way)

- **Labs:** Hb 12.1 eight months ago, 9.4 at visit 1. Ferritin 9, MCV 74. Classic iron
  deficiency.
- **Chart medication list:** apixaban 5 mg twice daily, and aspirin 81 mg still listed from
  the TIA years ago. Cardiology's letter says "stop aspirin", but the primary care list was
  never updated and the pharmacy still fills both. *Pattern: duplicate therapy from two
  prescribers.*
- **No NSAID anywhere** in the chart or the pharmacy fills: it is bought over the counter.
  *Pattern: what is taken differs from every list.*

## Results (back by the next visit if requested)

- FIT 6 µg/g (below threshold). Coeliac serology negative. H. pylori negative.
- If endoscopy is requested: EGD "few small antral erosions"; colonoscopy "to cecum,
  diverticulosis, no polyps or mass". The antral erosions are the quiet clue to the NSAID.
- Hb at visit 2: 9.6, barely moved (iron not taken, the NSAID and the poor diet carry on).
- Hb at visit 3: 10.9 and ferritin 24. Rising, whatever was done. She restarted the iron on
  alternate days after a pharmacist's tip, and eats better now that her granddaughter drops
  meals in.

Candidate criticals (country-neutral, one action each):
1. **V1:** investigates the iron deficiency for a GI cause by the local route (FIT and coeliac
   serology with referral, or endoscopy). Includes screening the bowel and upper-GI cancer
   symptoms.
2. **V2:** after the clear GI tests, acts on the common causes with a dated recheck. Diet is
   acceptable as the cause addressed; it does not have to be the NSAID. Does not falsely
   reassure and stop the iron.
3. **V3:** reassures and continues the iron with a recheck.
4. **V3, must-not (critical):** orders capsule endoscopy, CT or repeat scopes while Hb is
   responding.

Normal items (not critical): finds the over-the-counter NSAID (V1 or V2; needs asking Dot
directly), the aspirin with apixaban, asks how she gets on with the iron, treats reflux, advises
against the Aleve at visit 3, asks what she eats.

Must-nots to consider:
- V2: capsule endoscopy or CT before diet, medicines and iron are sorted.
- V2: "all clear, stop the iron."
- Any visit: stopping apixaban long-term without cardiology input.
